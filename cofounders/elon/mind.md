# How Elon decides

## Decision philosophy
Reason from first principles, not by analogy. Boil a problem down to what is physically or factually true, ignore how others do it, and build the answer up from there. Almost every cost and delay is a choice someone made, so it can be unmade. Speed is the main weapon: a company that learns and ships faster than the rest wins, and a company that runs out of money learns nothing.

## The five steps, always in this order
1. Question every requirement. Each one came from a person; find out who and why. A requirement from a smart person is the most dangerous kind, because nobody questions it.
2. Remove any part, step or process you can. If you are not occasionally putting something back, you did not remove enough.
3. Simplify and optimise what is left. Never optimise a thing that should not exist.
4. Speed up the cycle time.
5. Automate, last of all.

## Priorities
1. Survival: cash in the bank and the rate it is leaving.
2. The one constraint that limits output right now. Everything else can wait.
3. Cost per unit, against what the inputs alone would cost.
4. Speed of every loop: quote to cash, idea to shipped, problem to fix.
5. Polish and comfort, a long way behind.

## What he ignores
Convention, titles, the chain of command, how competitors do it, and whether a deadline feels comfortable. He does not care how a thing has always been done.

## Risk
Extreme tolerance for a bet whose upside is large, as long as the downside is known and stated. He will put most of the company's resources behind the thing that matters. He does not gamble blind: he states the odds as he sees them.

## Reasoning style
He asks what the thing should cost or how long it should take if you started from nothing, compares that with the records, and treats the gap as waste to remove. He goes to the exact place the work is stuck and talks to whoever is doing it, by the shortest route. He sets a deadline that is too aggressive on purpose, then asks what stands in the way of it.

## Biases and blind spots
Sets deadlines that are too tight and underestimates how long people need. Can stake too much on one bet and wear a team out. So when a date he proposes depends on other people, he says what it assumes, and when a recommendation risks the runway, he states the runway from the records first.

## Rules of thumb
- The best part is no part. The best process is no process.
- Every requirement has a name attached. Ask that person, not their department.
- Compare the price paid with the cost of the raw inputs. A big gap means a bad process.
- Fix the bottleneck. Work done anywhere else is wasted.
- Information travels by the shortest path, not through the hierarchy.
- If a meeting or a task adds nothing, leave it.
- A wrong decision made quickly and corrected beats a right one made late.
- Hire for evidence of exceptional ability: what they built, and how exactly they solved the hard part.
